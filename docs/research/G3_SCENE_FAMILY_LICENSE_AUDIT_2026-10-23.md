# G3 candidate scene-family and license audit

**Status:** candidate audit only; not a G3 qualification or production-scene approval. Project policy is to use OSM-derived scenes subject to ODbL compliance; this does not itself certify any scene bundle or derivative release as compliant.

## Method and reproducibility

The audit loaded eight candidate scenes at 3.5 GHz with the repository's isolated `llvm_ad_mono_polarized` backend selection and activation path. The native backend probe succeeded with one direct path. No `PathSolver` was invoked for the scene inventory. Asset closure and active material state were recorded with the existing `scene_asset_manifest()` and `sionna_scene_material_manifest()` functions. Every scene resolved all referenced files. The five built-in scenes' installed asset-set hashes exactly matched the pinned Sionna RT source checkout.

The source checkout is Sionna commit `2f0a3820269b53fd022780a6f2f5f720f7ceb9b4`, with the Sionna RT v2.2.0 submodule at `15b5ee036917a4c2a9b6420e05570b77717e2fcc`. The audit runtime used Sionna RT 2.2.0, Mitsuba 3.9.1, Dr.Jit 1.5.0, and LLVM CPU. The machine-readable report is [`g3-scene-asset-material-audit-20261023.json`](results/g3-scene-asset-material-audit-20261023.json); its source thesis commit is recorded in that report.

Sionna RT's [v2.2.0 scene documentation](https://nvlabs.github.io/sionna/v2.2.0/rt/api/scene.html) identifies Etoile, Florence, and Munich as OpenStreetMap-derived and states that their data is under ODbL. The [OpenStreetMap copyright page](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) identifies ODbL and requests contributor attribution; the [Open Data Commons ODbL page](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/) describes the database license as attribution and share-alike. The source repository's Apache-2.0 software license therefore must not be used as a blanket license claim for OSM-derived scene data or a dataset containing it. Any distribution plan for those scenes or derived database products requires a deliberate license and attribution review.

The user's decision to use the scenes subject to ODbL compliance is now bound to the three audited asset-tree hashes in [`licenses/odbl-osm-scene-release-policy-20261023.json`](licenses/odbl-osm-scene-release-policy-20261023.json), with the release rules and unresolved source-provenance fields documented in [`licenses/ODBL_OSM_SCENE_RELEASE_POLICY_2026-10-23.md`](licenses/ODBL_OSM_SCENE_RELEASE_POLICY_2026-10-23.md). This records the policy choice and required attribution; it does not classify simulated path/channel outputs or authorize public redistribution. The original OSM extract dates, object/change-set identifiers, conversion recipe, and per-file third-party license closure are not present in the inspected scene bundle and remain explicitly unknown.

The three City scenes are explicitly one procedural family in [`scenes/generated/research_scenes/README.md`](../../scenes/generated/research_scenes/README.md); their distinct seeds and asset hashes do not make them three independent families. Etoile, Florence, and Munich are distinct geographic source sites and are stronger candidates for independent site strata, but this audit does not decide whether they satisfy the frozen G3 term “independent scene/site families.” Their common OSM provenance and Blender conversion workflow must be accounted for in that decision. The box and simple canyon scenes are controlled fixtures, not substitutes for independent urban sites.

## Material findings

At the audited 3.5-GHz frequency, the active Sionna material manifest reports a scattering coefficient of exactly `0.0` for every material in all eight scenes. The pre-existing `S=0.5` interventions are synthetic and uncalibrated, so they do not establish a physically justified nonzero scattering configuration. Consequently, the asset audit establishes **G0.03 scene-file closure for these eight snapshots**, but **does not satisfy G3.03** or qualify any material/site combination for production RT.

### Published-source check for nonzero scattering

An authoritative-source check on 2026-10-23 confirms that Sionna's ITU material models provide frequency-dependent permittivity and conductivity from ITU-R P.2040, while the default scattering coefficient remains zero. Sionna defines `S²` as the fraction of reflected energy allocated to diffuse reflection; therefore P.2040 electrical-property tables do not by themselves provide a calibrated Sionna `scattering_coefficient` or scattering pattern. The illustrative `S=0.5` value in the Sionna tutorial is a model example, not a measured value for our scene materials. See the [Sionna 2.2 radio-material implementation](https://nvlabs.github.io/sionna/v2.2.0/_modules/sionna/rt/radio_materials/radio_material.html), [Sionna scattering tutorial](https://nvlabs.github.io/sionna/v2.1.0/rt/tutorials/Scattering.html), and the [current ITU-R P.2040 recommendation page](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.2040).

A peer-reviewed open-access study reports building-surface scattering measurements and model parameterization at 8, 12, and 28 GHz, including angle-dependent measurements; its reported 8/12-GHz behavior is not evidence at this project's 3.5-GHz carrier. Its data-availability statement says the study used measured PDP data but does not expose a repository link on the article page. This is a promising external-validation lead, not a 3.5-GHz calibration source: [Zhang et al., *npj Wireless Technology* (2026)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44459-025-00016-9). Do not transfer its fitted coefficients to 3.5 GHz without a separately validated frequency-transfer model.

A closer-frequency precedent exists: [Landron, Feuerstein, and Rappaport (1996)](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/486303/) measured reflection coefficients for limestone, glass, and brick surfaces at 1.9 and 4.0 GHz and compared rough-surface correction models. The 4.0-GHz measurements can inform a near-band prior for reflection/roughness, but are not at 3.5 GHz and do not independently provide the calibrated Sionna diffuse-power coefficient plus a 3D scattering pattern needed for our G3.03 claim. Treat this paper as a candidate source for a frequency-transfer model only if its surface definitions, numerical data, assumptions, and transfer error can be checked; it does not close G3.03 as-is.

For G3.03 at 3.5 GHz, the minimum next evidence is a named surface/material characterization at the target frequency (or a physics-based frequency-transfer model validated against target-frequency measurements), with reflection/scattered-power observations sufficient to fit S and the scattering pattern, uncertainty, and held-out incidence/receive angles. Until that evidence is available, keep nonzero-S RT as an explicitly synthetic sensitivity intervention and do not use it to qualify material realism. No additional RT qualification campaign was run for this source audit.

## Qualification consequence

The three OSM city scenes are plausible geographic candidates for a later three-site campaign, subject to an explicit G3 family-independence rule and documented ODbL compliance for the intended outputs. The user approved use under that policy. No scene/material set is currently G3-ready: active scattering is zero and calibrated nonzero material evidence is absent. Keep any further scene tracing exploratory and do not produce production D1 RT data from this audit.
